Your Role - Read This First
What a Success Architect Does (and Does Not Do)
This defines the line between what you own and what the customer owns.
A Success Architect IS responsible for:
  • Validating the solution architecture - confirming it is buildable on Salesforce, identifying gaps, flagging risks early.
  • Building a proof-of-concept or demo in a sandbox to prove the concept is technically sound before the customer invests in building it.
  • Writing the specification - exactly what to build, in what order, with what inputs and outputs. The customer's developer follows your spec.
  • Designing the Prompt Template - the AI instruction logic. This is your specialisation. You own the first draft.
  • Reviewing what the customer builds - testing, advising on quality, catching issues before go-live.
  • Asking the right questions - unblocking the team, surfacing decisions they haven't thought about yet.
A Success Architect is NOT responsible for:
  • Building the full production solution in the customer's org. That is their developer's job.
  • Maintaining the flows, LWC, or schema changes after go-live.
  • Making business decisions - which templates are approved, what "successfully resolved" means, who can see which templates. You surface these decisions; they make them.
  • Writing production Apex or LWC code - unless explicitly scoped into your engagement.
  • Owning the project timeline - Candace owns that. You own the technical quality of what you are responsible for.
The line in one sentence: You design the "what" and "how" and prove it works in a demo. Rohit's team builds it in production. You review and sign off that it matches the design.
Practical implication for this project: The Prompt Template and the Prompt Flow specification are yours to own. The Flow changes in their org, the LWC wiring, and the schema change are theirs to build using your spec. The questions section of this document is the tool you use to unblock them.

How to Guide the Customer - Step by Step
Your Conversation Playbook
What to say, what to deliver, and what to ask at each touchpoint. Follow this sequence.
1
Send a pre-meeting email - get the blockers out of the way
Before the demo meeting, email Rohit and Shankar to collect the four critical pieces of information you cannot proceed without. Frame it as "quick homework so our demo session is productive."
"Before our next session I'd love to show you a working demo. I just need three things from you: (1) 2–3 sample Email Templates - dummy data is fine, I just need to see the structure and merge fields. (2) A screenshot or export of your template folder/naming convention so I know how they're organised. (3) Which Case Status value means 'successfully resolved' in your org. Can you send these over by [date]?"
What you are unblocking: Without #1 and #2 you cannot write the EmailTemplate SOQL filter or the Prompt Template instruction. Without #3 your historical matching may pull the wrong cases.
2
Build the demo in a scratch or dev org - before the meeting
Do not wait for them. Once you have the sample templates, build the Prompt Template and extend the existing flow in a dev org. The demo is your proof that the architecture works.
What you build for the demo:
• Update Add_Historical_Case_Emails_to_Prompt2 - add Path A/B detection, add current case thread read, add EmailTemplate query for Path A, add TonePreference variable.
• Write the Prompt Template - Path A and Path B instruction blocks with tone handling.
• Use dummy Case + dummy EmailTemplate data to show both paths working end-to-end.
Do NOT build this in their production or sandbox org until they have reviewed and approved your demo. Always validate in your own org first.
3
Run the demo session (45–60 min) - show, then hand over
This is the pivotal meeting. Your goal is to show the working concept and get their sign-off on the architecture so Rohit's team can take it forward.
"I want to spend the first 20 minutes showing you the working demo - both paths. Then I'll walk you through what I've spec'd out for your team to build in your org. The rest of the time is yours - questions, adjustments, and we'll agree on what Rohit's team picks up next."
What you show:
• Path A demo: new credentialing Case → AI recommends template → fills merge fields → CSR picks tone → draft ready.
• Path B demo: existing Case with 3 prior emails (pending forms situation) → AI reads thread → drafts contextual follow-up → CSR picks tone → draft ready.
• Walk through the Prompt Template so they understand the instructions are transparent and editable.
• Show the flow to demonstrate Path A/B detection logic.
4
Hand over the specification document - this document
After the demo, send this guide to Rohit. The "Changes to the Existing Flow" table and the "Object Reference" table below are specifically written for their developer. You are not building it for them - you are giving them everything they need to build it themselves.
"I'll send over the full spec document after this call. It has the exact flow changes your developer needs to make - six additive changes, nothing gets deleted from the existing flow. It also has the Prompt Template draft and the questions we still need answers to. I'll stay available to review whatever your team builds."
What to attach:
• This implementation-guide.html (or a PDF export of it).
• The Prompt Template first draft (copy from the "Prompt Template" card below).
• The flow change spec table (in the technical section below).
5
Review what they build - do not rebuild it for them
Once Rohit's team has built it in their sandbox, schedule a 30-minute review. Your job is to test the output quality (does the LLM preserve template structure? does the tone control work?) and flag any deviations from the spec.
"Can you give me access to your sandbox once the flow changes are in? I want to run 5–6 test Cases through it - two new cases, two with existing threads, one with no history, one with a pending forms scenario - and validate the output before you move to production."
What you are testing: Does the LLM keep mandatory language intact? Does "shorter" not accidentally drop a required sentence? Does Path B not repeat information already sent?
6
Plant the Phase 2 conversation - but don't push it now
Once Phase 1 is live and the team has seen it work, introduce the idea of adding the custom field on EmailMessage to enable true frequency-based template scoring. Frame it as "the system getting smarter over time."
"Now that this is working, every time a CSR uses the AI draft, we can optionally record which template was used. Over 3–6 months that builds a dataset where the AI can say 'Template A closes this type of case 40% faster' rather than just picking by name match. That's a small schema change - one custom field on EmailMessage. Worth discussing when you're ready."
Technical Specification - Hand This to Rohit's Developer
Exact Changes to the Existing Flow
All 6 changes are additive. Nothing in the existing flow gets deleted or modified.
#ChangeWhereHowPhase
1 Add TonePreference input variable Flow variables panel New Text variable. isInput=true. Default value: standard. Values expected: shorter / standard / longer. LWC passes this in when CSR selects tone. Phase 1
2 Detect Path A vs Path B at flow start New node - insert before existing Get_Similar_Closed_Cases Get Records on EmailMessage WHERE ParentId = {!CaseRecord.Id} AND Incoming = false. LIMIT 1. Decision: record found → Path B. Not found → Path A. Phase 1
3 Path B: read current Case email thread New node - at start of Path B, before the existing historical case SOQL Get Records on EmailMessage WHERE ParentId = {!CaseRecord.Id}. ORDER BY MessageDate ASC. No limit. Loop through all records. Append FromAddress + MessageDate + TextBody to a new Text variable CurrentThreadContext. Assign CurrentThreadContext to the start of $Output.Prompt before the historical emails. Phase 1
4 Path A: query EmailTemplate candidates New branch after Path A/B detection Get Records on EmailTemplate. Filter by FolderName or DeveloperName containing the Case RecordType keyword (confirm naming convention with Rohit). LIMIT 5. Loop through results. Append each template's Name + Body to a Text variable TemplateCandidates. Assign to $Output.Prompt. Phase 1
5 Add output variables for winning template Flow variables panel Two new Text variables: WinningTemplateName and WinningTemplateBody. Both isOutput=true. LWC reads WinningTemplateName to display "Recommended: [name]" to the CSR. Phase 1
6 Tighten SOQL match: add Category + Sub-Category Existing Get_Similar_Closed_Cases node Add two more filter conditions to the existing SOQL: Category__c = {!CaseRecord.Category__c} AND SubCategory__c = {!CaseRecord.SubCategory__c}. Confirm exact field API names before applying. Phase 1
7 Add EmailTemplate__c custom field on EmailMessage Object Manager → EmailMessage Lookup field pointing to EmailTemplate. LWC writes this field when CSR stamps the draft. Flow reads this in Path A to count template usage frequency across historical cases. Phase 2
For the LWC developer (Rohit's team): Three LWC changes needed. (1) Pass TonePreference as an input to the flow when CSR selects shorter/standard/longer. (2) Read WinningTemplateName from flow output and display it as "Recommended: [name]" above the draft. (3) In Phase 2 - when CSR clicks Send, write the selected EmailTemplateId to EmailMessage.EmailTemplate__c.
Prompt Template - First Draft
This is yours to write and refine. Share this with the customer for review but do not ask them to write it.
You are a customer service email assistant for Allergan Moxie. Your job is to draft a professional outbound email for a Case. == CURRENT CASE == Case Number: {!CaseRecord.CaseNumber} Record Type: {!CaseRecord.RecordType.Name} Type: {!CaseRecord.Type} Reason: {!CaseRecord.Reason} Category: {!CaseRecord.Category__c} Sub-Category: {!CaseRecord.SubCategory__c} Status: {!CaseRecord.Status} Customer Name: {!CaseRecord.Contact.Name} Account: {!CaseRecord.Account.Name} Tone requested: {!TonePreference} ===== IF PATH = NEW CASE (no emails sent yet) ===== == CANDIDATE EMAIL TEMPLATES == {!TemplateCandidates} INSTRUCTIONS: 1. From the candidate templates above, select the one most appropriate for this Case Type, Reason, and Category. 2. State the template name you selected on the first line: "Using: [template name]" 3. Use that template as the structural foundation of your reply. 4. Replace all merge field placeholders (e.g. {!Contact.Name}, {!Case.CaseNumber}) with the actual values from the Case details above. 5. Do NOT remove, reorder, or paraphrase any mandatory language, form names, or legal statements in the template. 6. Apply tone: shorter = trim explanatory sentences only, keep all required content. longer = add empathy and context while keeping all required content. 7. Output only the final email body. No subject line. No commentary after the email. ===== IF PATH = EXISTING CASE (has prior emails) ===== == CURRENT CASE EMAIL THREAD == {!CurrentThreadContext} == HISTORICAL RESPONSES FROM SIMILAR CLOSED CASES == {!$Input:HistoricalEmails} INSTRUCTIONS: 1. Read the full email thread above carefully. 2. Understand the context: Is this a follow-up? Are there forms pending? Has there been a delay? 3. Draft the NEXT outbound reply that continues this conversation naturally. 4. Insert relevant Case attributes (status, form names, dates) where appropriate. 5. Do NOT repeat information already communicated in previous emails. 6. Apply tone: shorter = concise and direct. longer = warm and detailed. 7. Output only the final email body. No subject line. No commentary after the email.
This is a starting point. You will need to refine the instructions once you have seen real Email Templates from Rohit. The exact merge field syntax they use (e.g. {!Contact.Name} vs [Customer Name]) affects instruction #4 significantly.
