Why this email is written the way it is

As a Success Architect, your relationship with the customer is collaborative — not transactional. The email above is designed to do four specific things at once.

What the email doesWhere in the emailWhy it matters
Signals progress "I have been building out the demo and I am almost there." Prevents the customer from thinking you are stuck or waiting on them. You are showing you are already 80% done — they just need to complete the last 20%.
Removes anxiety about sharing "I am not asking for anything sensitive — no customer data, no production credentials." They have been reluctant to share templates. This directly addresses that reluctance before they even think it.
Makes each request specific and small Four numbered asks, each with bullet-point sub-questions and an explicit "a screenshot is fine." Vague requests get vague responses or no response. Specific asks with low barriers get answered. "2–3 examples, dummy data is perfectly fine" removes every excuse.
Explains the consequence of not answering The P.S. at the bottom. Most people do not answer because they don't understand why it matters. The P.S. explains it in non-technical terms — if I guess the wrong merge field syntax, the demo looks broken. That motivates action.

Tone notes

Keep it conversational, not formal. "Great meeting with you" and "I have built everything else" are intentionally informal. A stiff formal email signals distance. You want them to feel like you are on the same team, building something together.
Name what you have already done. "I have built the Prompt Flow, the Prompt Template with template selection logic, and three representative dummy email templates" — this is not boasting. It proves you are not blocked on your end and the request is genuinely the last piece.
Do not apologise for asking again. You have asked for this before and they have not delivered. Do not write "sorry to ask again" — that undermines you. Instead, the P.S. explains WHY you keep asking, which is much stronger than an apology.
Fill in the blanks before sending. Replace [last name] in the To/CC fields, [date] in the body, and confirm the email addresses. Sending with placeholders still in the email is worse than not sending it.

If they still don't share after this email

At that point you have done your job — you asked clearly, explained the consequence, removed every barrier. In the demo session, say this out loud:

"I want to be transparent — I built this demo using representative templates I designed based on the scenarios you described in our last meeting. The AI logic is correct and the architecture is sound. Once you share your actual template structure, I can update this in about 30 minutes and it will work exactly the same way with your real templates."

This positions you as thorough, not blocked. The demo still proves the concept. The template swap is a 30-minute task, not a rebuild.
